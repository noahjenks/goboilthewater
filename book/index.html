The Book

Go Boil the WaterA Partner's Guide to Supporting Homebirth

A book written from one man to other men, about how to show up well when your wife is having a baby at home.

Not a medical guide. Not a how-to manual. A presence guide — honest, practical, sometimes funny, and grounded in real experience from husbands, birth mothers, and the midwives who've seen it all.

Because the man in that room matters. And almost nobody is writing for him.

Chapter Outline

  1. Intro

    So… Yeah. A Man Writing a Book About Homebirth.

    Disarm the reader. Establish humility. Build trust. The story of how Noah got here — and why that makes him exactly the right person to write this.

  2. 1

    "Homebirth? That's a Thing?"

    A brief history of birth, how medicine changed everything, and why homebirth today isn't anti-medicine — it's selective wisdom. Meets the skeptical reader exactly where he is.

  3. 2

    Your Wife Says She Wants a Homebirth

    The conversation that brought you to this book. Normalize the fear, the confusion, and the resistance. And the fascinating tool called listening.

  4. 3

    But Is It Safe?

    The safety question is an emotion dressed as a question. Data, midwife licensing, transfer plans — and how to voice real concerns without becoming an obstacle.

  5. 4

    What Is "Home," Anyway?

    Homebirth isn't chaos — it's calm, intentional, and safe. What the environment actually looks like, and why your home isn't a liability. It's the entire point.

  6. 5

    Before the Room

    Show up to the appointments. Prepare the home. Handle the outside world. Get your own head right. The birth doesn't start when labor does.

  7. 6

    Go Boil the Water

    The history of a man's role in childbirth — and a redefinition of what "helpful" actually means. Presence over performance. The counterintuitive truth: doing less, better, is the job.

  8. 7

    Everyone Has a Position — Know Yours

    Who's in the room and why. What a midwife does, what a doula does, and how to stay in your lane so the people who know what they're doing can do it.

  9. 8

    How Do I Help When I Feel Helpless?

    Helplessness doesn't announce itself. Why it hits men so hard, how to recognize your own pattern before it fills the room, and a simple reset to get yourself back to steady when nothing is in your control.

  10. 9

    The Golden Hour

    The team disappears. The house goes quiet. Your job just changed. What the golden hour actually is — and how not to miss it by being somewhere else.

  11. 10

    Voices of Experience

    First-person voices from mothers, partners, midwives, and doulas — organized by theme. What helped, what didn't, what they wish the next man walking in already knew.

  12. End

    Conclusion: Go Boil the Water

    Not a checklist. A conviction. Show up, stay present, and make yourself secondary to the most important thing happening in the room.

Questions About the Book

Before You
Pick It Up

Who is Go Boil the Water for?

Husbands and partners whose wives have chosen to give birth at home, or are thinking about it. It's written man to man, for the person standing in the room who isn't sure what his role is supposed to be.

Is Go Boil the Water a medical guide?

No. It's a presence guide. It doesn't teach anyone how to give birth, and it doesn't replace advice from your midwife or doctor. It covers how a partner can show up well before labor, during the birth, and in the hours after.

Who wrote Go Boil the Water?

Noah Jenks, a husband, father of four, and former Head of School. He and his wife Katie have been through an adoption, two hospital births, and one home birth, and the home birth is what led him to write the book.

What is the book based on?

Noah's own experience, plus questionnaire responses from husbands, birth mothers, midwives, and doulas who have been through home births. Their voices appear throughout, and Chapter 10 is built entirely around them.

Does the book talk about home birth safety?

Yes. Chapter 3, But Is It Safe?, takes the question head on: what the data says, how midwife licensing works, what a hospital transfer plan looks like, and how to raise real concerns without becoming an obstacle.

Can I read a sample before I buy?

Yes. Chapter 1, Homebirth? That's a Thing?, is free. Enter your email on this site and you'll get the full chapter right away.

Is there an audiobook of Go Boil the Water?

Yes. The audiobook is available on Spotify, so you can listen on the way to prenatal appointments or while you get the house ready.

Where can I get the book?

The paperback and Kindle editions are available on Amazon, and the audiobook is on Spotify.
