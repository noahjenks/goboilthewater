From the Introduction

So… Yeah.
A Man Writing a Book About Homebirth.

Let's just get it out in the open: this is a little ridiculous. A man writing a book about homebirth — about something his body will never experience, something women have carried for generations.

And that's exactly the point.

I am wildly unqualified to explain childbirth — and deeply qualified to talk about what I wish I had known as a husband standing in the room, unsure, overwhelmed, trying to be helpful and often getting it wrong.

Our fourth child, Amani Rose, was born in a warm tub in our bedroom. I finally understood what people mean when they say birth is beautiful. I wish someone had walked me through this years ago. This book exists for the man I was then.

"Your role is to support. Say it again to yourself: my role is to support. Do this, and the game will go great."

— Go Boil the Water, Chapter 6

Common Questions

What Partners Ask
About Home Birth

What does a husband do during a home birth?

He supports. Not by running the labor or doing the midwife's job, but by staying close, steady and present. That means knowing who is in the room and what each person is there to do, managing his own anxiety so it doesn't become hers, and protecting the quiet hours after the baby arrives.

How can a partner prepare for a home birth?

Go to the prenatal appointments, help get the house ready, handle skeptical family and friends, and work through your own fears before labor starts instead of during it. The birth doesn't start when labor does, and neither does your role.

What is the golden hour after a home birth?

It's the quiet stretch right after the baby is born. At a home birth the midwife's team usually finishes up and leaves within a couple of hours, and suddenly the house is just yours. The partner's job shifts from supporting labor to protecting that time for his wife and new baby.
