## How the voice works (Noah, Nov 26, 2026)

- The caption formula is a checklist of what to cover, not a line-by-line layout. Write it as a few connected sentences that flow into each other, the way Noah would type it, not one clipped line per slot.
- The opening line is a real moment with her and him in it, often with what someone actually said ("She said 'I'm scared of the birth,' and he said 'you'll be fine.'").
- The normalizing line is warm and specific, and it often names Noah himself ("That was me. A lot of good husbands start there.").
- The tip is one full sentence with a concrete action and a "so he can" that says why it matters.
- The send line names a specific person ("Send this to the husband who learns by doing."), not a generic "send this to him".
- Talking-head scripts use HOOK, BEAT 1 to 3 and CTA. Each beat is one or two spoken sentences with a real detail from Noah's own life (the chair in the corner at Sebastian's birth, Mary Beth at Cora's birth, Gloria and the tub at Amani's). Short is fine. Clipped fragments strung together are not.
- Vary every carousel's CTA slide (tag and body) and the caption's CTA line across the week, tied to that carousel's topic. Keep the trigger word exact (CHAPTER or WORKBOOK). Never the same CTA wording twice in a week or two weeks running.
- Talking heads (Noah, Nov 27, 2026): put an INTRO line right after the hook, about 4 seconds, varied each time ("By the way, I'm Noah. I wrote Go Boil the Water for dads at home births. If he needs this, follow along."). Tell one full story with real names, places and words, about 110 to 150 words (35 to 45 seconds). End on one concrete thing to do. The CTA names exactly what is on offer (Go Boil the Water, the Before the Room workbook, or the $15 book-and-workbook pair for practices), never a vague "copies".
- Never pad with stock lines like "That's normal." or "Plain and simple." Say the actual thing.
