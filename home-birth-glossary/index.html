Home Birth Glossary

Home Birth Terms, Explained for Partners

The words you'll hear from midwives, doulas, and birth classes, in plain language, with what each one means for you.

Midwives and doulas speak a language most dads have never heard. Here's what the common terms mean and why they matter to the person standing next to the birth pool.

Active labor
The stretch of labor when contractions get longer, stronger, and closer together and the cervix opens faster. This is usually when the midwife is with you and your wife goes inward. Talk less, watch more. Read more
Birth plan
A written summary of your wife's preferences for labor and birth: who is in the room, lighting, pain relief, positions, and what happens right after. It's a guide, not a contract. When the birth asks for something different, protect her, not the plan. Read more
Birth pool
An inflatable tub filled with warm water for laboring and, if she chooses, giving birth. Do a test fill weeks ahead and make sure your water heater can keep up.
Braxton Hicks contractions
Practice contractions that can start weeks before labor. They tend to be irregular and usually fade with rest or a change of position. Real labor contractions keep coming and build over time. When in doubt, call the midwife.
Certified Midwife (CM)
A midwife with the same graduate education and certification exam as a Certified Nurse-Midwife, who comes from a background other than nursing. Licensing varies by state. Read more
Certified Nurse-Midwife (CNM)
A registered nurse with a graduate degree in nurse-midwifery. CNMs can work in hospitals, birth centers, and homes, can prescribe medication, and are licensed in all 50 US states. Read more
Certified Professional Midwife (CPM)
A midwife certified specifically in out-of-hospital birth, including home birth. CPMs are licensed in many US states but not all, so check the rules where you live. Read more
Counter-pressure
Firm, steady pressure on the lower back or hips during a contraction. It's one of the most useful things a partner can do with his hands. Ask the doula or midwife to show you exactly where to press. Read more
Doula
A trained support person who stays with the mother through labor, helping with comfort, positions, and breathing. A doula is not a medical provider and does not deliver the baby. A good one also coaches the partner. Read more
Early labor
The first part of labor, when contractions are milder and further apart. It can last hours or longer. This is the time to rest, eat, and get the room ready, not to start timing every contraction out loud.
Fourth trimester
The first weeks after birth, when mom is recovering and the baby is adjusting to the world. At home, the partner becomes the gatekeeper for visitors, meals, and rest. Read more
Golden hour
The first hour or so after birth, when the baby stays skin to skin on mom's chest. It helps the baby settle and start breastfeeding. At a home birth nobody comes in to interrupt it, so protecting it is the partner's job. Read more
Home birth (homebirth)
A planned birth at home attended by a qualified midwife, with a transfer plan in place in case hospital care is needed. Both spellings are common. Read more
Hospital transfer
Moving from a planned home birth to a hospital during or after labor. Most transfers are not emergencies. Common reasons include labor that stalls, exhaustion, or wanting pain relief. Read more
Low-risk pregnancy
A healthy pregnancy without known complications, such as twins, a breech baby, or a previous C-section. Home birth research and midwife screening focus on low-risk pregnancies. Read more
Placenta (third stage)
The organ that nourished the baby during pregnancy. It's delivered after the baby, usually within an hour, and the midwife checks it carefully. This is called the third stage of labor.
Postpartum
The period after birth. Home birth midwives usually check in within the first day or two and follow up over the following weeks. Read more
Prenatal appointments
Regular checkups during pregnancy. With a home birth midwife these are often longer and sometimes at your home. Partners who attend them tend to be calmer and more useful at the birth.
Pushing (second stage)
The part of labor after the cervix is fully open, when mom pushes the baby out. It can take minutes or hours. Your job is to stay close and keep your encouragement simple and personal. Read more
Skin-to-skin
Placing the newborn directly on a parent's bare chest. It helps regulate the baby's temperature and breathing and supports breastfeeding. Dads can do it too, once mom and baby have had their time. Read more
Transfer plan
The plan, made with your midwife before labor, for when and how you'd move to a hospital: which hospital, how far, by car or ambulance, and what would trigger the move. Know it cold. Read more
Transition
The final, most intense part of the first stage of labor, just before pushing. Women often feel overwhelmed or say they can't do it. It usually means the baby is close. Stay steady and keep believing in her.
Water birth
Giving birth in a birth pool. Many women also labor in the water for comfort and then move out to deliver. Your midwife will guide what's safe in the moment.
