How Does the Partner Take Care of Himself During a Long Labor?

By Noah Jenks, author of Go Boil the Water  ·  November 27, 2026  ·  6 min read

Quick answer

Plan for labor to be long, and look after yourself on purpose so you can stay with her. Pack real food and drinks for yourself, eat and drink something every few hours, rest when she rests in early labor, and agree ahead of time who covers you for short breaks. Your job is to still be steady at hour twenty, so taking care of yourself is part of taking care of her.

Key takeaways

  • Labor can be long, and the NHS says the early phase alone "can take hours or sometimes days."
  • Partners who skip food and water often run out of energy when she needs them most, and the NHS lists "not eating or drinking enough" among the causes of fainting.
  • Resting when she rests in early labor saves your energy for active labor and the birth.
  • Agree before labor who covers you for a few minutes when you need to eat, use the bathroom, or step outside.
  • Mahika, an international doula, asks fathers to take care of themselves so they can come back to center, and calls that part of being present.

When I asked dads in our survey what they'd do differently, Nathan didn't say anything about breathing techniques or the birth plan. He said this: "One thing I would change is making sure I eat more food and maintain my energy throughout the process. I barely ate during the entire labor, and I could feel my strength and stamina drain over time because of it."

His wife pushed for nearly three hours. He never left her side, and he's proud of that, rightly. He also ran on empty to get there.

Most of us walk into labor thinking about her and nobody else. That's the right instinct. But labor can be long, and the man who is still steady at hour twenty is the one who thought about himself a little at hour two.

How long can labor last?

Longer than most partners expect. The NHS says the early phase "can take hours or sometimes days." Your midwife is the one who tells you where she thinks labor is. Your job is to be ready for it to run long.

Kaelah, a mom from our survey, had one line she wished someone had told her husband: "It will likely be long! Be prepared." Another mom labored for about 40 hours. "I knew I would have to go with the flow of what happened but had no idea I would be in labor for 40 hours and certainly did not prepare mentally for that."

One dad described what that does to your sense of time: "I think when something so momentous is happening it bends time and space in a weird way." You'll look at the clock and not believe it.

Is it selfish to take care of myself during labor?

No. It's part of the job. Mahika, a doula I interviewed this fall, tells fathers to trust the team and be present, and then she gives them permission most men won't give themselves.

"If you need to self-regulate, if you need to breathe, if you need to nap, if you need to eat, please take care of yourself so that you come back to that center..."

Mahika, international doula

You can hear the rest of that conversation in Can a Dad's Stress Slow Down Labor?

The point is the center. Your wife needs the calm version of you. Hungry, dehydrated and wired on no sleep, you won't be him for long.

What should I eat and drink during labor?

Real food you can eat in a few bites, and water. Pack it before labor starts, because you won't think about it once things get going.

Todd Parker, whose daughter was born at home in Bali, spent hours bracing his wife through every contraction. "I didn't realize how physical it would be, even for me," he said. He also forgot to eat, which their doula had told them not to do on day one.

There's a practical reason to take this seriously. The NHS fainting page lists causes that include "standing up too quickly," "not eating or drinking enough," "being too hot," and "being very upset, angry or in severe pain." A warm birth room, a long day, and nothing in your stomach covers most of that list. If you're worried about fainting, read What If I'm Squeamish or Afraid I'll Faint at the Birth?

What I'd pack: sandwiches, fruit, nuts, leftovers from the night before, and a big water bottle that stays full. Set an alarm on your phone if you have to. Every few hours, eat something and refill the bottle. Bring her what she wants first, then eat yours.

Can I sleep during labor?

Often, yes, when she's resting and the team is fine with it. Early labor is usually the best window. For a home birth, I walked through that stretch in what the partner does during early labor, and resting when she rests is one of the jobs.

The people who attend births know this better than anyone. At Kristen Nagle's home birth, one of the women attending went off to do a Sudoku and take a nap. "Sometimes there's just, you've gotta wait," Kristen said. "There's nothing to do." Her husband Chris took his calm from theirs.

Sleep doesn't always come. One mom told me that at hour 20, "the doula had him try to take a nap." Then she added, "Spoiler alert: he couldn't sleep." Lying down for twenty minutes with your eyes closed still counts. Tell your wife where you'll be, keep your phone on, and get up the second she wants you.

Who covers for me when I need a break?

Decide that before labor. A doula, the midwife's assistant, a sister or friend your wife has chosen, anyone she's comfortable with for a few minutes. Then when you need to eat, use the bathroom, or step outside for air, you're handing off, not walking out.

Tell her when you're going and when you'll be back. Then come back when you said you would.

This matters for her too. One mom from our survey said that late in her labor, "I just needed someone to tell me what to do or focus on. My sister did that a lot during my labor." In a long labor, nobody can be sharp for every hour. A team that takes turns keeps someone sharp for her.

How do I stay steady over the long haul?

Take small resets before you need a big one. When you feel the panic or the fog coming, drink some water, take a few slow breaths, and come back to her. I lay out a 60-second version in how to stay calm when you feel helpless during labor.

And don't measure yourself by how much you did. At our first two births, in the hospital, I mostly tried to stay upright. At our home birth, our midwife Gloria sent me to the kitchen because the birth tub wasn't filling fast enough. I had a job, and I was there when Amani came. Being there at the end is the part that counts.

After the birth, you'll have a newborn and a tired wife, and the work keeps going. Eat something then too. The next stretch is the first week at home, and that has its own jobs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long can labor last?

It varies a lot. The NHS says the early (latent) phase alone "can take hours or sometimes days." One mom from our survey labored for about 40 hours. Your midwife is the one to tell you where she thinks labor is.

Is it okay for the partner to sleep during labor?

Yes, when she's resting and the midwife or doula is fine with it. Early labor is often the best time. Tell her you're lying down, keep your phone on, and get up the moment she wants you.

What should the birth partner eat during labor?

Real food that's easy to eat in a few bites, like sandwiches, fruit, nuts, and leftovers, plus plenty of water. Pack it before labor starts so you don't have to think about it, and eat something every few hours.

Is it selfish to take a break during labor?

No. A short break to eat, drink, or use the bathroom keeps you steady for her. Arrange cover first, with the doula, the midwife's assistant, or a friend she's chosen, and tell her when you're going and when you'll be back.
